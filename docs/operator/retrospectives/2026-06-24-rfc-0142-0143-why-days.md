## TL;DR

The two RFCs were slow for **two different reasons**, and only one is a process defect:

- **0142 P4 (~36h)** was slow because the design was **genuinely hard and the gate was
  genuinely rigorous**: a `falsification_gate` ran **v1→v9**, and *every* cycle caught a
  distinct, source-verified, build-breaking defect (C1/C2/C3 → N1 → BC-N1/BC-N2 →
  M1/M2 → M3/M4 → M5 → M6 → M7). None re-surfaced. The slow was **earned**; the
  deliverable is correct because of it.
- **0143 (~45h, ~10 cycles)** was slow because of the opposite — the gate **chased the
  same structural root (the shared-`striatum-lane`-uid forgeability, `BC1-W1-ORACLE`)
  at finer and finer grain for ~5 cycles before naming it architectural** (BC1 →
  CHANNEL → TOKEN → CAPTURE → ORACLE), then the "fast decoupled escape" (Slice A)
  **re-hit the identical root** across two more gate cycles. There was no tripwire to
  say "this is the same wall — stop and make an architecture decision."

Cutting across **both**: an **operator-paced revision ratchet**. The gate's revision
model is "a `needs_revision` ends the run; the operator banks the dialogue and
hand-scaffolds a fresh `-vN` run." Across the two interleaved chains that was **~16
manual bank+scaffold turns**, and roughly **half the calendar time was the loop idling
for the operator** (e.g. zero commits 06-23 09:50→17:27, a ~7.5h daytime gap; a
superseded v6 "8.6h run" was mostly idle). Measured gate **compute** was ~10–13h vs.
~32–45h **wall-clock**. This is the dominant, tracked process cause (**#587**).

## The data

- **0142 P4 design:** 9 gate cycles, 7 distinct defect families, 0 re-surfacing, 2 build
  attempts (attempt 1 wedged on the `go/**` write-scope footgun, **#586**; attempt 2's
  review caught a serve-bricking 0021-embed regression, fixed). Cleared `accept_with_findings`.
- **0143 design:** ~10 gate cycles total (original v1→v7, Slice-A v1 ×2, Slice-A v2),
  the original chain re-deriving the *same* `BC1` root at finer grain every cycle.
- **Idle vs compute:** per-cycle compute ~60–135 min; a 9- or 10-cycle convergence is
  ~10–13h of legitimate work. The remaining ~20–30h of calendar was operator latency
  between cycles (banking + re-scaffolding + session hand-offs).

## Root causes (ranked, evidence-cited)

1. **[dominant, tracked] Operator-paced revision ratchet.** Each `needs_revision` =
   operator banks dialogue + hand-scaffolds a fresh `-vN` run. ~16 turns across the two
   chains; ~half the calendar was idle (commit-gap evidence above). *Tracked by* **#587**
   (gate-native auto-bank/rescaffold for clean revision cycles).
2. **[0143-specific] The structural obstruction was discovered late.** The shared-uid
   wall was visible by ~v3–v4 (first same-uid `/proc` replay) but not called
   architectural until v7 — ~4 cycles drilling one wall. No same-core-recurs tripwire.
   *Proposed:* **#588**.
3. **[both] Genuine convergence rigor (0142) / deep inherent complexity.** 0142's 64-cell
   decoupled boot-path decision table + owner-bundle watermark + DDL-revoke ordering;
   0143's same-uid authentication. This is the *good* slow for 0142; for 0143 it
   compounded #2.
4. **[0143] The split created an RFC-0168 dependency tail.** Slice B is gated on RFC
   0168 (#585), itself mid-design (v2 in flight) — half of "RFC 0143" is deferred to
   another RFC's full lifecycle.
5. **[0143] The "fast decoupled escape" was not free.** Slice A re-hit the same root
   across 2 gate cycles and an operator manual-clear on a weaker (observability-only)
   invariant. *Proposed:* **#589**.
6. **[both] One runner footgun + lockstep interleaving.** `write_scope: go/**` matched
   nothing → silently wedged a complete 0142 build attempt (**#586**); the two chains ran
   interleaved against one operator, each waiting on the other's turn. *Proposed:* **#591**,
   and sequencing folded into #587.

## What worked (keep doing)

- **The falsification gate caught real, build-breaking defects before any code.** On
  0142 it earned its `accept` across 9 cycles. On 0143 it correctly refused to clear a
  forgeable floor twice — had we direct-built the v1 design, we'd have shipped a floor
  that **never fires on the real #512 path** (under-fire) and **over-fires on a forged
  tmux exit**. The gate is the reason the landed Slice A is honest about being
  best-effort/RFC-0168-bounded rather than falsely claiming forge-resistance.
- **Sealed bubblewrap verifier receipts** (`builtin:go-build` / `builtin:go-vet`) gated
  both integrations — verification independent of the implementing agent.
- **Daemon-routed recovery, never hand-finishing a wedged run** (e.g. the 0142 v9
  wrap-up `artifact_immutable_mismatch` was cleared via `recovery resolve-blocker` +
  `run cancel`, D262), preserving provenance.

**Bottom line:** rigor was not the enemy — the gate's findings were real and the landed
code is correct because of them. The recoverable waste was **(a)** the human-paced
revision ratchet (#587) and **(b)** ~4 cycles spent drilling one structural wall on 0143
before naming it (N1/N2). Fix those two and a "days" RFC of this shape becomes a
"hours-of-compute, one-operator-session" RFC.
